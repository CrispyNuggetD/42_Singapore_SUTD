In new five_goal_1, P1 eats at 1000 ms alongside P3: the proposed handoff
does occur. But P5 last ate at 600 ms, misses another meal, and dies at 1400 ms.
Thus fixing the P1/P5 handoff does not establish fair repeating phases.

MATHEMATICAL ISSUE WITH THE CURRENT POLICY
The helper selects order from floor(completed_meals / N) modulo 2 once
before acquiring forks. Waiting workers retain their old selected pointers.
A change in the total does not make those workers reselect, nor establish
that every philosopher completed one meal. Multiple order generations coexist.

order_counterexample.txt gives a reachable abstract interleaving ending in:
P1 holds F1 -> needs F2
P2 holds F2 -> needs F3
P3 holds F3 -> needs F4
P4 holds F4 -> needs F5
P5 holds F5 -> needs F1

P2/P4 selected the old order; P1/P3/P5 selected the reversed order.
At this state the total is 6 and individual counts are [2, 1, 2, 0, 1].
The model omits timing and death detection: it disproves an unconditional
fork-order deadlock guarantee, not claims a deadlock occurred in native tests.
No native run in this set hung after reporting death; those failures were starvation.
